02 · Live experiences

Sauvage — a fragrance you can turn in your hand

A fragrance page told as scenes. The bottle gathers itself out of ink, walks the scent's own story — desert light, bergamot, pepper — and ends standing on your table.

  • There is no photograph of the bottle anywhere on the page. It is a real object in 3D, turning as you scroll, with the label always facing you.
  • The bottle is lit to match every backdrop it moves through. It sits inside the photograph instead of floating on top of it.
  • Point a phone at the floor and the bottle is there, at its real size. Nothing to install — it opens straight from the browser, on iPhone and Android.

03 · Film

Two product films, made without a shoot

Play · 1:41

One hundred and one seconds for a French door refrigerator. The doors open, cold air moves through the cabinet, water pours into a glass. No camera, no studio, and no sample shipped across the world.

  • Every part on screen was checked against the maker's own product photos — the drawers, the water tank, the back wall, the dispenser.
  • Shots a camera cannot get: the air traveling through the cabinet, a cut straight through the door, the filter coming apart in layers.
  • Measured, not eyeballed. In the first take the water left the dispenser 30 pixels off its center. Nobody would have named it; everybody would have felt it. The final film holds it within four.
  • Voiced in Spanish for the Peruvian market, with English subtitles on the player, and scored to the cut.

05 · How it works

Inside your team, start to finish

You bring the work you've sold; I produce it — design and build — under your flag or mine. Inside your team: your channels, your standups, your deadlines — remote, on US-aligned hours.

3

Under whose flag: white-label production for your clients, or credited work under mine — agreed in writing. What never changes: the reasoning ships with the piece, so the person defending the work in the room is never defending it empty-handed.

  1. Diagnose. Measure the current state — numbers, not impressions.
  2. Propose. The concept arrives with its written argument.
  3. Build. Written by hand, nothing bolted on.
  4. Check. Accessibility measured, tested on the smallest phone, page weight counted.

Handoffs arrive complete — design, build, and the written reasoning — so nothing needs a second meeting to be understood.

This site is held to the same standard as the work.
It loads fast on a phone, works for screen readers and holds together at any size — and every one of those claims was measured, not assumed. The numbers are in the note.

4

The figures: Lighthouse 100 · 100 · 100 on desktop and 97 on mobile, zero AA contrast failures across 272 measured elements, zero layout shift, proven at 320 px, no framework. Measured 2026-08-16 against the live site: Lighthouse 12 (performance, accessibility, best practices) on emulated mobile and desktop, and this studio's own audit tooling — every text element's contrast computed against the background actually painted behind it, at 320, 768 and 1440 px. The mobile 97 is the hero type loading on throttled 4G. Re-measured after every change that could move it.
