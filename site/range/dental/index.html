01 · Why the paper comes first

Most people are not afraid of the drill. They are afraid of the bill.

We have never met anyone who put off a crown because they enjoy toothache. They put it off because nobody would tell them what it costs, and because the last office gave them a number after the work was done.

So we moved the paper to the front. You get the estimate at the chair, printed, with the procedure codes on it — the same codes your insurer uses, so you can call them and check us. Then you decide. Including deciding not to.

03 · Your first visit, in order

Ninety minutes, and you leave knowing everything we know.

  1. Paperwork before you arrive

    We send two pages by text the day before. Nobody should be filling in a clipboard with a sore tooth.

  2. Images and a full chart

    A set of X-rays and a tooth-by-tooth chart, read out loud while a hygienist writes it down. You hear the same words we do.

  3. Impressions, if we need models

    Alginate, two minutes in the mouth, poured in stone the same afternoon. The models stay here for two years in case something changes.

  4. The estimate, printed and handed over

    Everything found, everything it costs, what your plan covers, in what order it should be done and what can wait. On paper.

  5. Cleaning, if today is the right day for one

    If your gums need the deeper kind, we say so instead of doing the quick version and calling it done.

08 · Questions people actually ask

The ones that come up at the front desk, answered here instead.

Do I have to have the work done the day you find it?
No, and we will not phone you about it. The estimate is good for ninety days. If something is genuinely urgent — a cracked cusp, an abscess — we will say so plainly and explain what happens if you wait, which is the honest version of pressure.
What if I have not been to a dentist in years?
Then you are a normal first visit here. Nobody will comment on it, and the plan we write will be in stages, cheapest and most urgent first, so you are not looking at one enormous number.
Can I pay over time?
For anything over $500 we split it into three payments in-house, no interest and no credit check, first one on the day of treatment. We do not sign anyone up for a medical credit card.
My child has never been to a dentist. What happens?
The first appointment is a chair ride, a count of the teeth and a sticker. No X-rays unless there is a reason. If they are not ready that day, we stop and try again in six months — that is not a wasted visit, it is how it is supposed to go.
Do you take Medicaid or BadgerCare?
For children, yes. For adults we are not currently enrolled, and rather than leave you calling around, the front desk keeps a short list of offices nearby that are, and will hand it to you.
Is the whitening any good, or is it a gimmick?
Custom trays work; the light in the chair mostly does not, so we do not sell it. Trays and gel are $265 and they work on natural teeth only — a crown or a white filling will not change colour, and if that is going to show, we will tell you before you pay.
I am terrified. Genuinely.
Say so when you book and we put you first thing in the morning, allow double the time and agree a stop signal before anything starts. Nitrous is available and it is $79. Plenty of people here started that way and now come in for cleanings without thinking about it.
