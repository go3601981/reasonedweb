01 · How it works

Four things, and none of them is a sales call.

This is a room with barbells in it. What you are paying for is the equipment, the space around it, and a coach who is on the floor rather than in an office.

Pay by the month

Cancel by text, any month, and it stops at the end of it. There is nothing to sign and nobody will ask you why.

Barbells, not machines

Eight platforms, twelve racks, dumbbells to 120 lb. No cable stacks and no televisions on the wall.

A coach is always here

Not a personal trainer waiting to be booked — somebody on the floor whose job is to watch the room and say something when it matters.

Everything is written down

Your sets go on a card in a box by the door, in your handwriting. That card is the whole product, and you can take it with you if you leave.

03 · What the coach actually does

Watches. Says one thing. Goes away.

The job is not to count your reps or shout. It is to see the set that is about to go wrong, say the one cue that fixes it, and then leave you alone — which is what most people want and almost nobody sells.

  • Every coached hour is on the schedule by the door, with a name on it.
  • If you would rather train without being corrected, say so once and that is that.
  • Nobody here sells supplements, and nothing is for sale at the desk.
  • Injured or coming back from surgery? Bring the sheet from your physio and we will work around it, not through it.

04 · If you have never touched a barbell

Then this page is written for you, not for the person already doing it.

Nobody starts strong and nobody here cares what you can lift on the first day. What the on-ramp does is teach five movements properly, in a group of four, so that you can walk into the room afterwards and know exactly what to do without asking.

You will not be the weakest person in here. Somebody started last Tuesday, and the person on the next platform is lifting an empty bar on purpose.

  • Six weeks, twelve sessions, four people, $195 and it includes the month.
  • Squat, press, deadlift, row and carry. That is the whole syllabus.
  • Wear anything with flat shoes. There is no dress code and no mirror wall.

08 · Questions we get at the desk

Answered here so you do not have to ask them out loud.

Can I just come and look?
Yes, any open hour, and nobody will follow you around with a clipboard. If you want to train while you are here it is $18 for the day, and that comes off your first month if you join within a week.
Is this one of those gyms where everyone is enormous?
No. The average member is in their forties and started here knowing nothing. There are strong people in the room, and the reason they are pleasant to train beside is that they remember being new.
What if I can only come twice a week?
Then the programme gets written for twice a week. Two honest sessions beats four that you resent, and a coach who tells you otherwise is selling attendance rather than training.
Do I need my own shoes, belt and all that?
No. Flat shoes and clothes you can move in. There are loaner belts and straps in the bin by the racks, and if you get to the point where your own kit would help, somebody will tell you which and why.
How do I cancel?
Send a text saying you want to stop. It stops at the end of that month and you are not charged again. No form, no phone call you have to make in business hours, no retention offer.
Is it busy?
Between 5 and 7 PM on a weekday you may wait for a rack, five minutes at worst. Before 8 AM and on Sunday the room is close to empty. We cap the membership rather than sell past what the floor holds.

Come in once. That settles it faster than this page can.

Eighteen dollars, no appointment, no tour unless you want one. If it is not your kind of room you will know inside ten minutes, and that is a fine outcome.
