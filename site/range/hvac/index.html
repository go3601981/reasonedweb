The jolt that gets the compressor turning. It measures fine at 80° and gives up at 130°, which is why this is the most common call we take.

Why it matters 43° of difference

A system built for Chicago moves twenty degrees. Yours moves forty-three.

At 118° outside and 75° inside, everything in the machine runs closer to its limit than the people who designed it ever tested. Heat kills parts here, not age — a component that measures perfectly in a garage at 80° can be out of tolerance on your roof at 130°.

So we measure at the temperature the system actually runs at: in the afternoon, with the unit under load. A reading taken at nine in the morning tells you almost nothing about four in the afternoon.
