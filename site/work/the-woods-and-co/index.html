Case study

The Woods & Co — bicoastal PR

A single-page concept for the beauty-and-wellness PR firm: placements as proof, and every claim written so it survives being checked.

Unsolicited concept. Not affiliated with, or commissioned by, The Woods & Co.
01 · Diagnosis, verified by hand

Ten tiles, clicked ten times

The concept began with verification, not opinion. The live site's ten case-study tiles do not open anything — I clicked all ten. The Instagram section renders empty: a heading and 36 pixels of height. Two fully written team bios sit in the page's code, switched off. And the Dolder Grand client mark in circulation has an anniversary badge sitting across the wordmark — so the concept leaves it out of the client wall rather than show it broken.

1

Each finding is stated as what I verified, never as an accusation. The point of hand-checking every tile isn't to catalogue faults — it's that a redesign built on unverified assumptions inherits them. What I found broken shaped what the concept fixes; what I found working was kept.

02 · The argument

Promoted, not invented

“A recognized leader in beauty, health and wellness” was already on the firm's site. I did not write a new slogan; I promoted the one they had already written. A PR firm is hired for one thing, so the outlets they place clients in sit in the first screen — nothing has to be scrolled to before a visitor knows what you get. And the wall of thirty-plus client marks moved up to sit right under the numbers, on white, with no color filters — the marks are drawn to sit on white, and anything else fringes them.

2

Attribution stayed exact everywhere: the firm's “18 years” is the founder's experience, not the company's age, so the page attributes it to her every time it appears. Press roundups are credited to their source. A claim that survives being checked matters more here than anywhere — checking claims is this client's profession.

03 · Proof, consolidated

Three moments saying one thing became one

Testimonials, industry roundups and Instagram used to be three separate moments saying the same thing. They are now one social-proof section — with the client quotes kept letter-for-letter, including the pause in the middle of one of them, because a quote tidied up is a quote changed.

On mobile, a “Get in touch” button rides the bar the whole way down and the phone number is styled as something you tap — on a phone there is no such thing as hovering to find out.

04 · Typography

Inherited, then stepped

The system keeps what the live site already loads — Crimson Text for body, Montserrat for labels — and adds one step of hierarchy: Libre Caslon Text for headlines, at its true regular weight.

3

Caslon's synthetic 600 weight was refused: a browser fakes a bold by thickening strokes, and a faked weight falsifies the face. Playfair Display was rejected as the beauty-sector reflex since 2016; Cormorant for fragility at 17 px. The cheapest respectful move was to keep the client's own faces and give them the one step they lacked.

05 · Standards

Measured on the painted background

Contrast gets measured rather than eyeballed, on the background actually painted: the ink used for section numbers had to darken, and the form fields — white on off-white, effectively invisible — got a defined edge. All 113 blocks of text pass AA at both tested sizes. The layout is checked down to a 320-pixel screen, the menu opens and closes with a keyboard as well as a finger, and the main button sits inside the first screen on a small laptop as well as a large one.
