Work / Ulric de Varens
Case study

Ulric de Varens — US storefront

A full storefront redesign for the French perfume house's US shop — ulricdevarensusa.com at the time of diagnosis.

Client engagement — Ulric de Varens USA
01 · Diagnosis, measured

The brand was hiding its own color

Before proposing anything I measured what existed. Across the nine brand files supplied, I counted every color instruction: 270 of them, and 237 — 87.8% — are gray, black or white. Meanwhile the catalog itself disagrees: the dominant hue of ten catalog photographs lands on eight different hues. A house whose bottles run the spectrum was being staged in grayscale.

The shop carried operational debt too: a Best Sellers tab showing placeholder cards, one product with no photograph and no price, the six most premium items reading as sold out, and a “43 years of passion” claim that had quietly become 45 — a number that ages on its own is a claim that expires without telling you.

1

Counting beats opining. “Your brand is too gray” is taste; “237 of your 270 color instructions are grayscale while your own catalog spans eight hues” is a finding the client can verify without trusting me. Every diagnosis on this page works that way: measured against the material itself.

2

Why inventory states belong in a design diagnosis: a storefront's credibility is the sum of its weakest card. Placeholder products titled “Example product title” at $19.99 do more damage than any typography choice can repair — so the redesign had to include the operational fixes, not just the visual ones.

02 · The substrate

No color chosen where one can be sampled

The redesign stands on a graphite stage. A neutral stage is the only one that lets every bottle be its own color — and it means the house can add the next fragrance in any color at all without repainting the store.

Every accent color in the concept is sampled, not picked: the graphite of the case, the chrome of the UDV signature, the Prestige gold measured across three bottles. Under each numbered fragrance sits a block of colored glass sampled from its own bottle — the fastest way a shopper tells six identical silhouettes apart, and the same way they are told apart on a shelf.

3

This is the identity of the whole engagement in one rule: where a value can be derived from the client's own material, deriving it beats choosing it. A sampled palette is defensible in front of any judge — the judge can hold the bottle.

03 · Hierarchy and commerce

The bottle leads, the face does not

The hero is a product gesture, not a model's face — a face with no bottle near it could belong to any house. One hard limit governs the fold: the main button has to remain visible without scrolling.

Prestige, a women's collection, opens the page — the client's own data, not my assumption. The buy button wears the Prestige gold: the color that says “buy” is the color already sitting on the most expensive thing the house sells. And each product card gained its olfactory note, copied word for word from the client's own fragrance sheets — the number one reason a person picks one bottle over another, and it costs nothing to publish because the client already wrote it.

4

“Original Creation” became the lead claim for the same reason: it answers the exact assumption a shopper makes on seeing a French perfume under twenty-five dollars. Every headline slot went to the claim that neutralizes an objection, not the one that flatters the brand.

04 · Typography

A Didone that earns its place

Display type is Bodoni Moda. The argument is on the bottle: the extreme thick-to-thin of a Didone is the same gesture as the UDV signature, and the Didone is a French neoclassical form for a house that leads with “Made in France”.

5

The rejections carry as much reasoning as the choice: Playfair Display reads as a template because everyone reaches for it; Cormorant is fragile at small sizes and is saturating this sector. A typeface decision you can defend includes the faces you turned down and why.

05 · What was refused

Integrity constraints, kept visible

  • Payment marks are never redrawn — a card mark is a trademark, and an approximation of one is not a shortcut
  • Apple Pay withheld until the client confirms it is actually offered
  • No invented ratings or reviews — those belong to the platform that collected them
  • Every claim on the page traceable to the client's own material

A concept that fakes its trust signals is rehearsing the client's future liability. Refusals are design decisions too, and they ship with the same written reasoning as everything else.

06 · Standards

Measured, not assumed

The layout holds down to a 320-pixel screen. Every tap target clears the accessibility minimum, keyboard focus is always visible, and the tabbed collection works with arrow keys. Every pairing of type and background is measured against the background it is actually painted on — at phone width and at desktop width, every block of text measured clears the threshold.
